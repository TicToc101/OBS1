
**Opción B

The foundation of Tito's Table begins with mastering your core products—yogurt, kefir, and parfaits—while systematically building the brand loyalty and capital required to open a full-scale restaurant.

**The Tito's Table Expansion Roadmap**

|Phase|Milestone|Focus|Why This Matters Now|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Phase 1: The Lean Launch**|Micro-Batch Production & Direct Sales|Perfecting recipes (yogurt, kefir, oat bowls), building a core audience, and securing a commercial kitchen or cottage food license.|Keeps overhead low. You validate that people will pay for your specific flavor profile before signing a risky commercial lease.|
|**Phase 2: Growth & Systems**|Wholesale, Catering & Subscriptions|Partnering with local gyms or offices, offering weekly parfait subscriptions, and codifying all recipes into strict SOPs.|Generates predictable, recurring revenue to build capital. SOPs ensure quality remains identical even when you hire your first prep cook.|
|**Phase 3: The Pop-Up Transition**|Physical Presence Validation|Hosting weekend pop-ups, selling at premium farmers' markets, and testing your first coffee and breakfast pairings.|Bridges the gap between packaged goods and hospitality. You learn health department regulations and customer flow for open-air food service.|
|**Phase 4: Brick & Mortar**|Coffee Shop & Restaurant|Securing a retail lease, building out a commercial kitchen, and launching a full café menu centered around your dairy foundation.|Realizes the long-term vision. You enter this phase with an existing customer base, proven recipes, and the cash flow to survive the first year.|

**Executing Phase 1**

- **Establish the Core Product Matrix:** Instead of offering twenty items, perfect three to four hero products. A signature Greek yogurt, a probiotic kefir, and a customizable oat bowl.
    
    - _The Mentor's "Why":_ Complexity kills startups. Every new ingredient you introduce adds waste, storage costs, and prep time. Mastering a small matrix ensures high margins and operational efficiency, which is critical when you eventually transition to a bustling coffee shop.
        
- **Codify Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs):** Document every gram, temperature, and fermentation hour. If a batch of kefir needs 24 hours at a specific temperature, write it down immediately.
    
    - _The Mentor's "Why":_ Right now, the business is solely in your head. To scale to a restaurant, you must be able to hand a binder to a newly hired prep cook and get the exact same yogurt you make yourself.
        
- **Secure Legal and Production Foundations:** Determine if you will operate under local cottage food laws (selling from home) or rent space in a shared commissary kitchen. Secure liability insurance and necessary food handler permits.
    
    - _The Mentor's "Why":_ A single health code violation can permanently sink a food brand. Operating strictly by the book from day one builds trust with the premium customers who will one day frequent your cafe.
        
- **Build the "Beta" Customer Base:** Start selling to a closed group of friends, family, and local community members. Solicit brutal feedback on texture, sweetness, and packaging integrity.
    
    - _The Mentor's "Why":_ Friends will buy once out of support, but strangers only buy if the product is excellent. This beta group helps you tweak the acidity of your yogurt or the crunch of your granola before you spend money on broad marketing.
